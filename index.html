How do wind and weather show up in Finland’s day-ahead electricity price?

Wind explains up to two fifths of the price variation.

An end-to-end data platform on Azure and Databricks. Azure Functions fetch three open APIs every hour (Fingrid, ENTSO-E and the Finnish Meteorological Institute) into a landing zone. Auto Loader brings the data into a Unity Catalog lakehouse, and dbt models it into a tested star schema. Every resource is in Terraform, and GitHub Actions deploys it with OIDC instead of secrets.

The data covers every quarter-hour since January 2024. Each extra gigawatt of wind lowers the price by 12 to 17 €/MWh. Prices go negative 5% of the time, mostly on windy summer nights, and the most expensive quarter-hours are cold, calm weekday mornings when Finland imports.

It ran on Azure from 20 October to 3 November 2026 for about 9 € a month, and then I took it down so it costs nothing. The next step would have been a price forecast on top of the gold layer.
